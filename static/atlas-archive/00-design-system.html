Atlas — Design System

Built, not
decorated.

Atlas looks like a well-set financial newspaper crossed with a terminal. Editorial serif for anything human, monospace for anything measured, and nothing rounded anywhere. This page is the reference for extending it — including the mascot.

01 — Palette

Six colours, used sparingly

Ink
#0B0B0B
Canvas. Near-black, never pure black.
Paper
#F2F0EA
Text and inverted blocks. Warm — deliberately not #FFFFFF.
Acid
#C8FF2E
The signature. One accent per screen, never decorative.
Mute
#6B6B66
Labels, captions, secondary data.
Negative
#FF5C47
Losses, errors, destructive actions only.
Positive
#4CAF7D
Gains. Muted so it never competes with acid.

Hairlines are rgba(242,240,234,0.12). Lifted surfaces are rgba(242,240,234,0.08). There are no drop shadows and no gradients, except a subtle angular one inside progress rings.

02 — Typography

Serif for people, mono for numbers

INSTRUMENT SERIF · 52PX · 92% LEADING Good morning
INSTRUMENT SERIF · 21PX Gym — push day
GEIST MONO MEDIUM · 10PX · 1.2PX TRACKING 01 — WEATHER
GEIST MONO · TABULAR £14,283.40

Never SF Pro. Numerals are always tabular so columns align. Labels are always uppercase and tracked. Body copy barely exists — if something needs a paragraph it usually needs redesigning.

03 — Form language

Zero radius, everywhere

DO

Hard edges. Mitred joins. Straight lines wherever one will do. Flat fills. 1px hairlines instead of borders or shadows.

DON'T

Rounded corners, pills, capsules, soft shadows, gradients, glassmorphism, or anything that looks like a default iOS control.

DO

Treat data as texture — grids of squares, bar columns, dot matrices, thin full-bleed rails.

DON'T

Use illustration, mascbots, emoji, or decorative iconography where a shape or a number would carry the meaning.

The chess pieces are the clearest expression of this: drawn as geometry, not glyphs.

04 — Mascot brief

Assemble it, don't draw it

The identity is geometric and anti-decorative. A soft, rounded, friendly creature would fight everything above — it would look bolted on. What fits is something that reads as constructed.

  • Built from squares, rectangles and triangles only. No curves, no rounded corners.
  • Acid on ink, or a paper outline with acid detail. Two colours maximum.
  • Should look like it could be drawn with a ruler and set square.
  • Flat. No shading, no gradients, no highlights.
  • Belongs on graph paper, not in a children's book.
  • Works at 16px as a favicon and at 1024px as an app icon.

Reference points that fit the language: the knight above, a stacked cairn, an angular constellation, a surveyor's mark, a folded paper form. Something with the character of an instrument rather than a pet.

Test: if it can't be described as assembled rather than drawn, it's probably wrong for Atlas.

05 — Voice

Plain, British, unhurried

  • British English. “Organise”, “colour”, “£”.
  • Say the thing plainly. No exclamation marks, no “Oops!”, no “Awesome!”
  • Empty states explain what to do, not that something is missing.
  • Estimates are labelled as estimates. Ranges, not false precision.
  • Never claim certainty the data doesn't support.
